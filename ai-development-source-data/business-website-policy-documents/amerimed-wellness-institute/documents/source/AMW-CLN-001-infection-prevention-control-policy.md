## 1. Purpose and authority
This policy sets out how Amerimed reduces the risk of healthcare-associated infections (HAIs) to patients, staff and visitors. It is based on:
- the CMS Conditions of Participation (42 CFR 482.42)
- CDC/HICPAC guidelines
- the Cal/OSHA Aerosol Transmissible Diseases Standard (Title 8, Section 5199)
- the OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR 1910.1030)
- California SB 739 / SB 1058 HAI reporting laws
- Oregon and Washington HAI reporting rules

The **Infection Prevention Committee** (meets monthly) has authority to institute any control measure when there is a risk of transmission. This includes closing units to admissions.

## 2. Standard precautions (all patients, all the time)
1. **Hand hygiene:** alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR) or soap and water at the **5 Moments**: before touching a patient, before a clean or aseptic procedure, after body-fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching patient surroundings. Soap and water is required for *C. difficile* and norovirus, and when hands are visibly soiled.
2. **PPE** chosen on anticipated exposure (gloves, gown, mask, eye protection).
3. **Respiratory hygiene / cough etiquette:** masks at entrances for symptomatic people.
4. **Safe injection practices:** one needle, one syringe, one patient. Single-dose vials are used for one patient only.
5. **Sharps safety:** engineered safety devices are required. Needles are never recapped.
6. **Environmental cleaning** with EPA-registered disinfectants, following contact times.
7. **Reprocessing of reusable medical equipment** following manufacturer instructions for use (IFU).

## 3. Transmission-based precautions

| Precaution | Examples | Key requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | MRSA, VRE, CRE, *C. difficile* (Contact-Plus), scabies | Gown and gloves on room entry; dedicated equipment; sporicidal cleaning for *C. diff* |
| **Droplet** | Influenza, pertussis, *N. meningitidis* (first 24 h of therapy), mumps | Surgical mask within 6 feet; private room preferred |
| **Airborne** | Tuberculosis, measles, varicella, disseminated zoster | Airborne Infection Isolation Room (AIIR, negative pressure, ≥12 ACH); fit-tested N95 or PAPR |
| **Enhanced (COVID-19 and novel respiratory pathogens)** | SARS-CoV-2, novel influenza | N95, eye protection, gown and gloves; AIIR for aerosol-generating procedures |
| **Special pathogens** | Ebola, Marburg, MERS | Activate the Special Pathogens Plan; transfer to the regional treatment centre (Amerimed Pacific Medical Center is the designated Assessment Hospital - illustrative) |

Isolation signs are posted at the doorway. Precautions are discontinued only by Infection Prevention or under nurse-driven protocols.

## 4. Hand hygiene monitoring
- Target compliance: **≥ 95%**, measured by electronic monitoring systems and trained secret observers (minimum 200 observations per unit per month).
- Results are posted monthly on unit huddle boards and reported to the Board Quality Committee.

## 5. HAI surveillance and targets

| Measure | Definition | FY2026 target (SIR) |
|---|---|---|
| CLABSI | Central line-associated bloodstream infection | < 0.50 |
| CAUTI | Catheter-associated urinary tract infection | < 0.60 |
| SSI | Surgical site infection (colon, hysterectomy, hip, knee, CABG) | < 0.75 |
| MRSA bacteremia | Hospital-onset | < 0.60 |
| *C. difficile* | Hospital-onset LabID event | < 0.55 |

Data is reported to CDC's **National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN)** and is publicly reported by CDPH. SIR = Standardized Infection Ratio.

## 6. Prevention bundles
- **Central lines:** insertion checklist, chlorhexidine skin prep, maximal sterile barriers, daily necessity review, CHG bathing in ICUs.
- **Urinary catheters:** insertion only for approved indications; nurse-driven removal protocol; bladder scanner first.
- **Surgical site:** pre-op CHG bathing, nasal decolonisation for orthopaedic and cardiac cases, weight-based antibiotic prophylaxis within 60 minutes of incision, normothermia, glucose control.
- **Ventilator-associated events:** head of bed raised ≥ 30°, daily sedation interruption, oral care with a toothbrush every 12 hours.

## 7. Antimicrobial stewardship
A multidisciplinary Antimicrobial Stewardship Program (ASP), led by an ID physician and an ID pharmacist, does prospective audit and feedback, prior authorisation for restricted agents (e.g. carbapenems, daptomycin), IV-to-oral conversion and annual antibiogram publication. This meets the requirements of CA Health & Safety Code 1288.8.

## 8. Employee health
- Required immunity and screening: see AMW-HR-001.
- **Exposure to blood or body fluids:** wash immediately, report to Employee Health or the ED within **1 hour**, and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is offered within 2 hours where indicated.
- Staff with fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, draining lesions or a new rash must not work until cleared ("Don't Come To Work Sick" policy, with protected sick leave).

## 9. Outbreak management
An outbreak is suspected when cases exceed baseline (e.g. 2+ epidemiologically linked cases of the same organism). Infection Prevention will:
1. confirm the case definition and line list
2. implement control measures
3. notify the local public health department (**within 24 hours** for reportable outbreaks under 17 CCR 2500)
4. communicate with staff, patients and families
5. complete an after-action review

## 10. Construction and renovation
An **Infection Control Risk Assessment (ICRA 2.0)** is needed before any construction, renovation or maintenance that disturbs ceilings, walls or water systems. Water management follows ASHRAE 188 to prevent *Legionella*.

## 11. Visitors
Visitors with symptoms of infection are asked to postpone. During declared respiratory seasons, masks may be required in patient-care areas. Children under 12 may be restricted from high-risk units (NICU, BMT, ICU) during outbreaks.
