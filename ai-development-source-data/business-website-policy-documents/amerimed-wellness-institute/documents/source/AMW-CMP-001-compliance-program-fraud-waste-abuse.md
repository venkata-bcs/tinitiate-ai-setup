## 1. Program structure
Amerimed's compliance program follows the HHS Office of Inspector General **General Compliance Program Guidance (November 2023)** and the seven elements of an effective compliance program:

| Element | Amerimed implementation |
|---|---|
| 1. Written policies and procedures | Code of Conduct; 140+ compliance policies on PolicyNet |
| 2. Compliance officer and committee | Chief Compliance & Ethics Officer reports to the CEO and directly to the Board Audit & Compliance Committee |
| 3. Training and education | Annual general training; role-based coding, billing and research training |
| 4. Effective lines of communication | Integrity Line (24/7, anonymous); open-door policy |
| 5. Enforcing standards | Consistent discipline; compliance performance in evaluations |
| 6. Risk assessment, auditing and monitoring | Annual risk assessment and work plan; external coding audits |
| 7. Responding to problems and corrective action | Investigations, root cause analysis, refunds and self-disclosure |

## 2. Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS)
It is a felony to knowingly and wilfully offer, pay, solicit or receive **anything of value** to induce or reward referrals of items or services payable by federal healthcare programmes (42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b)).
- All arrangements with physicians and referral sources must fit a **safe harbour** where possible, be in writing, be at fair market value and be approved by Legal and Compliance.
- Patient inducements (waivers of copays, free gifts) are restricted. Nominal-value items are limited to **USD 15 per item / USD 75 per year** per patient (per OIG policy).
- Routine waiver of copays and deductibles is prohibited. Financial-need waivers follow AMW-PAT-003.
- **California** (Business & Professions Code 650), **Oregon** and **Washington** have similar state laws that apply to all payers.

## 3. Physician Self-Referral Law (Stark)
Physicians may not refer Medicare patients for designated health services (DHS) to an entity with which they (or an immediate family member) have a financial relationship, unless an exception applies (42 U.S.C. 1395nn). This is a **strict liability** law, so intent does not matter.
- Every physician arrangement is tracked in the **Contract Management System** and checked against an exception (e.g. employment, personal services, fair market value, non-monetary compensation up to the annual CMS limit).
- Medical directorships require timesheets before payment.
- Expired agreements are flagged 90 days before expiry. Payments stop on expiry unless the agreement is renewed.

## 4. False Claims Act (FCA)
Knowingly submitting false or fraudulent claims to federal programmes leads to treble damages plus per-claim penalties (adjusted annually; about USD 14,300 - 28,600 per claim). Examples include:
- billing for services not rendered or not medically necessary
- upcoding (e.g. billing a level-5 visit for a level-3 service)
- unbundling
- duplicate billing
- billing for services by excluded or unlicensed providers
- retaining overpayments for more than 60 days after identification

**California False Claims Act** (Government Code 12650) and the **Washington Medicaid Fraud False Claims Act** have similar provisions, including qui tam (whistleblower) rights.

## 5. Billing and coding integrity
- Coders hold AHIMA or AAPC credentials and are audited at least each quarter. The accuracy target is ≥ 95%.
- Clinical documentation must support the level and medical necessity of every service billed. Copy-forward and cloned notes are monitored.
- Teaching physician rules (42 CFR 415.172) apply to services involving residents.
- Overpayments are investigated promptly and repaid within **60 days** of identification (with a 6-year look-back).

## 6. Exclusion screening
All workforce members, medical staff, vendors and contractors are screened before engagement, and **monthly** after that, against:
- the OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE)
- the GSA System for Award Management (SAM)
- the Medi-Cal Suspended & Ineligible Provider List, the Oregon Medicaid exclusion list and the Washington HCA exclusion list
- state licensure boards

## 7. Conflicts of interest
Board members, officers, physicians in leadership roles, purchasing staff and researchers file **annual conflict-of-interest disclosures**. Research conflicts follow 42 CFR Part 50 Subpart F (PHS-funded research).

## 8. EMTALA compliance
Every person who comes to an Amerimed emergency department receives an appropriate **medical screening examination**, and stabilising treatment or an appropriate transfer, regardless of ability to pay or insurance status. Registration may not delay screening. Central log and on-call lists are maintained.

## 9. Reporting and investigations
- Report concerns to a supervisor, to the Compliance Office (compliance@amerimedwellness.example, 408-555-0190) or to the **Amerimed Integrity Line: 1-888-555-0198**.
- Investigations start within **5 business days**. Findings and corrective actions are reported to the Audit & Compliance Committee.
- Where appropriate, Amerimed uses the **OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol** or the **CMS Voluntary Self-Referral Disclosure Protocol (SRDP)**.

## 10. Non-retaliation
No one will be retaliated against for reporting in good faith. This protection includes qui tam relators under 31 U.S.C. 3730(h). Retaliation is grounds for termination.

## 11. Record retention
Billing and medical records are kept for at least **10 years** (or longer if state law, a minor's age of majority plus the statutory period, or litigation holds require). Medicare Advantage records are kept for 10 years under 42 CFR 422.504.
